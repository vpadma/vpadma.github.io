# Artwork and anatomy references

This atlas uses published illustrations with original pixels preserved. The web interface adds target markers, viewport crops, and removable label covers. Those overlays are adaptations for interactive study, not new anatomical drawings. Source labels can be restored in learning mode; quiz mode always covers them. Context labels that do not name quiz answers may remain.

## Gray’s Anatomy (1918)

[Figure 591](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gray591.png), Henry Vandyke Carter / Henry Gray, is public domain. Its historic “coronary vein” label refers to the left gastric vein. This plate is provided as a supplementary view of gastric drainage, not a modern dissection photograph.

## Interpretation notes

- The course PDF’s first heading is “Principal Arteries of the Human Body”; the requested “Abdominal Aorta” tab includes all 30 listed arteries.
- The PDF’s “External Carotid Srtery” spelling is corrected.
- “Arcuate artery” is interpreted as the dorsal foot vessel. The supplied PDF does not distinguish the foot artery from renal arcuate arteries. Confirm the intended lab model with your instructor.
- “Phrenic vein” is interpreted as inferior phrenic.
- The generic palmar arch term includes superficial and deep arches. The interactive target identifies the superficial arch.
- The accessory hemiazygos target is the upper left paravertebral segment in the exposed thoracic venous plate. Its connections vary; see [CT Findings of Azygos Venous System (2024), Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10864146/).
- The posterior interventricular artery’s origin is variable. The course term specifies the common right-dominant pattern.
- Source colors are preserved. Portal plates use colors to distinguish tributaries; those colors do not imply different oxygen concentrations.

A sourced illustration does not by itself verify an interactive target. Marker placement and quiz behavior require separate visual review and browser QA.
